## Caveats that decide how much the table can carry
* The suites were written around the layers' threat classes, so Horizon coverage is by construction high. A scanner losing here says the scanner was not built for that class.
* `B3` for HIEB is a three-pattern emulation. Do not cite it as evidence about checkov, Trivy, Prowler or any CSPM product.
* Checkov caveats from P36: fixtures were written by the InfraGuard author in InfraGuard's own annotated format; this favours InfraGuard; checkov does not parse this format (frameworks listed per row), so most checkov 'no findings' are UNSUPPORTED, not PASS; checkov covers a far larger space (Terraform, Kubernetes, CloudFormation, secrets, ...) that InfraGuard does not attempt.
* HMIB has **no unsafe-serialization (pickle-style) scenario**, the one model-artifact class that model-file scanners do cover; the 0/16 for scanners reflects that omission as much as any weakness of scanners.
* Nothing here measures false-positive rates on large real corpora.

## Verdict
Incremental novelty is **supported only** for (a) release control that binds an exploit-oracle result to a signed permit and (b) classes with no mature-scanner counterpart (model provenance, cross-agent channels, receiver-anchored forensics), and **only on synthetic fixtures**. It is **not supported** for infrastructure-posture detection, where the one real-tool comparison was format-limited and inconclusive, and the breadth of mature CSPM tools is far greater.
