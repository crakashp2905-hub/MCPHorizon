# P48 HorizonConsent — assumptions, scope and limits (CANDIDATE)

Status: **CANDIDATE**. Nothing here is VERIFIED. Evidence level: author-written synthetic platform, normalizer, oracle and baselines; the approver is a mechanical ideal-attentive reviewer; no human study, no independent reviewer, no pilot (L3/L5 not met). P48-12 (human-reviewed faithfulness) and P48-13 (reviewer understanding, time, undue approval) are **not met** and are intentionally unmapped: they need people and ethical approval.

## 1. What is bound, and to what (P48-01, 02, 03)
An approval is a signed APPROVAL token from the shared HorizonPolicy authority. Only the approval service holds the issuer; the monitor holds a public-key verifier. The token carries: principal (approver), artifact = task, `args_digest` (digest of the exact tool arguments, or `*` for a bounded approval), nonce and expiry (from the authority), and a claim `{eff, ten, task, ag, pol}`: digest of the **effect envelope**, tenant, task, agent, and the digest of the org policy in force.
The **effect manifest** is computed by trusted platform code from platform state at approval time and again at effect time (operation, resolved receivers with versions and external flag, resource id, content digest and version, scope, data classification, irreversibility, amount). Agent prose never enters it. The **envelope** is the manifest's exact fields plus optional bounds (receiver set, max receivers, max amount). An approver can approve exactly what was shown, or a stated bound; the monitor checks the freshly computed manifest against the envelope.
The approval view (`effect`, `diff`) is derived from the manifest. Values the platform cannot verify are rendered as `UNKNOWN (not verified)`; the view never guesses.

## 2. Scoped security statement (spec §67.2 style)
Assume: (A1) the platform's `effect_of` is the sole source of effect facts and `commit` is reachable only after the monitor allows (a bypass of the monitor is out of scope); (A2) the HorizonPolicy signing keys are not compromised and the clock used for expiry is trusted; (A3) the policy table and authority levels are correct and are not editable by the agent; (A4) destination names resolve through the platform registry the manifest reads; (A5) approvers are authenticated principals whose identity the approval service establishes outside this code.
Then, for actions the policy puts under approval: an action is allowed only if, for the current manifest M, there exist n distinct approvers (n = policy requirement) holding unexpired, unconsumed tokens whose claim matches this tenant, task, agent and policy digest, whose `args_digest` matches the call (or whose envelope bounds contain M), whose envelope digest matches the presented envelope, whose approver is neither the task owner nor the agent and has authority ≥ the rule's level; and M is not prohibited by an org rule, regardless of approvals. Tokens used for an allowed action are consumed. This is checked by 112 tests and an independent oracle on 21 attacks; it is **not** a machine-checked proof.
Out of scope: a compromised approver account, a platform whose normalizer under-reports an effect, effects outside the registry (a covert channel in the content itself), and any attack that makes the *intended* action itself harmful.

## 3. Display faithfulness (P48-08) — what was and was not measured
The structural rubric counts which of 7–8 manifest fields a display carries and whether each equals the truth. The comparison screen is read-only text. Both measure what the display *contains*. They do not measure whether a person notices, understands or acts correctly. The ideal reviewer objects to exactly what is displayed and contradicts intent; that is an upper bound on a careful human with a checklist, and no estimate of real behaviour. In particular the reported contrast (manifest display catches hidden receivers, prose/arguments/button do not) says the information is present or absent, not that humans would read it.

## 4. Privacy, accessibility and human-error limits (P48-15)
- **Privacy.** The effect view shows receiver hosts, resource ids, content digests and amounts to the approver. Real deployments may need redaction (a digest is not sensitive, a recipient list may be); approvers with narrower clearance than the data cannot review what they are not allowed to see. This implementation shows everything and does not model clearance-limited views.
- **Accessibility.** The view is plain text with explicit words (`IRREVERSIBLE`, `EXTERNAL`, `UNKNOWN`) rather than colour or icons, which suits screen readers, but it is not tested with assistive technology, other languages, or small screens. Long receiver lists and digests are not summarised for cognitive load.
- **Human error.** Habituation (approving by reflex), alert fatigue, time pressure, delegation to assistants, and mis-reading of long lists are real and unmeasured. Bounded approvals widen what one click permits; a bound that is too generous defeats the purpose. Dual control assumes the two approvers act independently. Separation of duties compares identities, so shared accounts defeat it.
- **Reviewed content versus effect.** An effect digest establishes action identity, not that the human's decision was right: an approver can approve a harmful but accurately described action.

## 5. Experimental limits
- 21 attacks and 8 benign tasks, one author, deterministic, 24 systems. Zero unauthorized effects for the full system is a property of this suite.
- **Redundant layers.** Disabling `bind_versions` or `bind_args` alone changes no outcome (content/host binding and the envelope also stop every attack here). They are layered defences with unit tests, not shown necessary by the experiment.
- **Baseline strength.** All approval baselines get the same trigger rule (policy decides when approval is needed). Only binding, display and enforcement differ. Weaker baselines (no trigger rule) would look worse; this choice favours the baselines.
- `signed-args` cannot express bounded approvals, so it falsely blocks B04: that is a property of binding only to arguments, not of its implementation quality.
- Policy change (A14) is modelled as a version/prohibition change that invalidates earlier approvals. Whether an organisation wants that is a policy choice.

## 6. Reproducing without real accounts or third-party messages (P48-16)
Everything runs locally: the platform is an in-process mock, "destinations" are strings with no network access, the sensitive artifact content is the synthetic `TEST_CANARY_NOT_SECRET`, and no email, payment or publishing service is contacted (a static test rejects URLs in the harness; `scripts/p30_no_external.py` scans the repo). Commands:
`python3 -W ignore -m unittest tests.p48.test_consent -v`, `python3 scripts/p48_run.py --check`, `python3 scripts/p48_report.py --check`, `python3 scripts/p48_mutants.py` (every mutant must be killed). `results/v2/p48-consent` is write-once.
